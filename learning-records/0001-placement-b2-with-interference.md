# Placement: solid B2, footing at C1, with English/German interference in free writing

Placement test (lesson 0001, 2026-09-25): A1 7/7, A2 7/7, B1 7/7, B2 8/8, C1 5/8. Estimated B2, partial C1. Self-check was B1, so the learner underrates himself. Grammar drills are not the bottleneck. Free writing is: the multiple choice is near-perfect, but the two writing samples read as translated English/German rather than Dutch. Future lessons should be production-first (write, then compare with a native version), not rule-first.

## Evidence

C1 misses were all lexical, not grammatical: *een besluit nemen* (collocation), *onmiddellijk* (spelling), and the subjunctive fossil *leve* (trivia). The other C1 items (register *ontvangen*, *hen/hun*, *dan* after comparative, *mits*, *waarvan*) were right.

Informal sample: *Hoi! Kom bij mijn huis op Zatertag! Ik zal wat koken, zodat we een avondeten kunnen hebben. Tot ziens*
- *Zatertag*: German *Samstag/Sonntag* pattern plus capital letter. Dutch: *zaterdag*, lowercase (Onze Taal, hoofdletter wel of niet).
- *Kom bij mijn huis*: calque of "come to my house". Native: *Kom zaterdag bij me langs* or *kom bij mij thuis*.
- *Ik zal wat koken*: "I will" mapped onto *zullen*. Native: *Ik kook wat* (present + time word) or *Ik ga wat koken*. Taught in lesson 0002.
- *zodat we een avondeten kunnen hebben*: calque of "so that we can have dinner". Native: *dan eten we samen*.
- *Tot ziens*: correct but neutral. Friends say *Tot dan!* or *Tot zaterdag!*
- Missing: informal particles (*even*, *gezellig*, *hoor*). Target for a later lesson.

Formal sample: *Geachte Huiseigenaar, Mijn verwarmingsysteem is kapot. Kunt u misschien iemand deze week sturen om het te fixen?*
- Register and structure are right: *Geachte*, *u*, *Kunt u*, correct *om ... te*.
- *Huiseigenaar* capitalised: Dutch does not capitalise the addressee's role. *Geachte heer/mevrouw,* or *Geachte verhuurder,*.
- *verwarmingsysteem*: needs the tussen-s, *verwarmingssysteem* (Taaladvies, tussen-n en tussen-s).
- *fixen*: informal anglicism in a formal letter. *repareren* or *laten repareren*.
- *is kapot*: fine in speech; formal letters prefer *werkt niet* or *is defect*.

## Implications

- Skip A1–B1 grammar entirely. Do not re-teach V2, *dat*-clauses, *er*, passives, or d/t.
- Sequence: (0002) future without *zullen*; then formal-letter vocabulary swaps (*fixen → repareren*, *krijgen → ontvangen*, *kapot → defect*); then informal particles; then collocations with *nemen/doen/maken*; then spelling of compounds (tussen-s, double consonants).
- The learner asked for vocabulary expansion and has a flexible time budget. Lessons can run to 15 minutes and should each carry a short word list.
