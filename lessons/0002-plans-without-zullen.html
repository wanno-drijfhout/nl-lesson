Why this lesson

Your placement test put you at B2 with footing at C1. The grammar drills were near-perfect. The writing gave you away. You wrote:

Ik zal wat koken, zodat we een avondeten kunnen hebben.

Every word is Dutch. The sentence is English. A Dutch friend would write:

Ik kook wat, dan eten we samen.

The gap is not grammar. It is one habit: English "will" and German werden make you reach for zullen. Dutch almost never does. Fix this one habit and your invitations, texts and emails all sound a level more native.

The rule

Say when, then use the present tense. That is the everyday Dutch future.

Morgen kook ik. Tomorrow I'll cook.
Zaterdag komt ze. She's coming on Saturday.
Ik bel je vanavond. I'll call you tonight.

The time word goes first, so the verb comes second and the subject follows it. You already do this. The ANS lists this as a normal function of the present tense: the action lies after the moment of speaking.

Two helpers, each with a job

HelperJobExample
gaan + infinitiveAn intention, a decision, or something about to start. Best with action verbs.Ik ga wat koken. Het gaat regenen.
zullen + infinitiveA promise. A suggestion (Zullen we…?). A hope or a guess.Ik zal het niet vergeten. Zullen we vrijdag gaan? Het zal wel goed komen.

Source: Taaladvies.net, Gaan / zullen (algemeen). It names three cases where gaan is always fine: moving somewhere to do something, the start of a new action or state, and a plan or decision. Everywhere else, standard Dutch uses zullen or the present.

Netherlands-only trap. Do not put gaan in front of zijn, hebben, worden, kunnen, moeten. Say Volgend jaar word ik dertig. In Belgium ga ik dertig worden is fine. In the Netherlands it marks you as not from here.

Ukrainian hook. You already say Завтра я їду до Києва with a present-tense verb for a fixed plan. Dutch does that for almost everything. The trap runs the other way: Ukrainian буду and English "will" both tempt you into zullen.

Ten time words to put first

You asked for vocabulary. These ten make the rule usable. Say each out loud with a verb: Straks bel ik. Morgen kook ik.

straks later today
zo meteen in a moment
vanavond tonight
morgen tomorrow
overmorgen day after tomorrow

zaterdag Saturday. Lowercase, no "op"
volgende week next week
over een uur in an hour
binnenkort soon
later later on, in life

Days and months are lowercase in Dutch: Onze Taal, hoofdletter wel of niet. Your Zatertag was German twice over: the capital and the -tag.
