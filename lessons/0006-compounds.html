Why this lesson

In the placement test you wrote verwarmingsysteem. One s short. You also missed onmiddellijk. Both are the same kind of slip: a long Dutch word is built from parts, and each part keeps its letters. English writes "heating system" as two words, so your hand wants a space or drops a letter at the seam. This lesson gives you three rules. They cover almost every compound you will ever write.

Ukrainian hook. Ukrainian glues words too, with a linking vowel: пароплав, землетрус, водоспад. Dutch does the same thing, only its glue is s or e(n). English is the odd one out here, not Dutch.

The idea

1. One thing, one word. Onze Taal: a basic rule of Dutch spelling is that compounds are written as one word. rug + zak = rugzak. Then rugzaktoerisme. Then rugzaktoerismebranche. No spaces, however long. People go wrong in the long ones, like voetbalinterland. Onze Taal, samenstelling

2. Write the s you say. There is no hard rule for the linking s. If you pronounce an s at the seam, write it. If you do not, leave it out. Often both are fine: spellingregel and spellingsregel. Taaladvies, Onze Taal

3. Cannot hear it? Swap the second part. When the second part starts with s, your ear is no help. Swap it for a word that does not start with s. You say stationsplein, so you write stationsstraat. You say verwarmingsketel, so you write verwarmingssysteem.

4. Hyphen when vowels crash. If the seam puts two vowels together that would read as one sound, a hyphen is required: na-apen, diploma-uitreiking, media-aandacht. Onze Taal, klinkerbotsing
