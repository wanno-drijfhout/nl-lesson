Why this lesson

Your informal invitation in the placement test was grammatical and cold. It had no particles: the small unstressed words like even, maar, eens, toch, hoor that carry the speaker's mood. The ANS, the reference grammar of Dutch, calls them schakeringspartikels and says they add nothing to the facts and everything to the intent. Natives sprinkle them into every spoken sentence. A learner who never uses them sounds like a form letter. This lesson gives you six, with one job each.

Ukrainian hook. You have these too: ж, же, -но, хіба, таки. Дай-но is close to Geef eens. Same layer of language, different words.

One sentence, five moods

This table is lifted straight from ANS 8.3.2. Same command, five particles, five different speakers.

SentenceANS labelFeel
Geef die boeken hier.bevelAn order. Blunt.
Geef die boeken eens hier.vriendelijk verzoekA friendly request. "Would you pass me…"
Geef die boeken maar hier.geruststellend en vriendelijk verzoekReassuring. "Go ahead, just hand them over."
Geef die boeken nou hier.met ongeduld geuit verzoekImpatient. "Come on, hand them over."
Geef die boeken toch hier.met ergernis of ongeduld geuit verzoekAnnoyed. "Just give me the books already."

The six, one job each

ParticleJobExamples
evenMakes an action small and quick. "Just", "a sec". The friendliest softener for requests and plans.Ik bel je even. Kom even langs. Hou even je mond, wil je? (ANS)
eensTurns a command into a friendly request. Often with even: eens even.Kijk eens. Laat die kinderen eens met rust. (ANS)
maarGives permission or reassurance. "Go ahead", "feel free". Also in Laten we maar gaan.Kom maar binnen. Zeg het maar. Laten we maar gaan. (ANS)
tochMid-sentence: irritation or insistence, "as I told you". At the end with a question mark: "right?", asking for agreement.Ik heb je dat toch gisteren al verteld! (ANS) Je komt zaterdag, toch?
hoorSentence-final. Softens or reassures, like "you know" or "don't worry". Very Dutch, very informal.Gezellig, hoor. Geen probleem, hoor. Sorry hoor.
welAffirms against a doubt. The opposite of niet. With a guess: "probably".Ik kom wel. Het komt wel goed. Dat zal wel.

The five-mood table and the examples marked (ANS) are from ANS 8.3.2. The ANS also notes these words are always unstressed and can never open the sentence as particles. Even hield hij zijn mond is a different word: the adverb "briefly". Hoor is not in the ANS list of particles: grammars file it as an interjection (Wiktionary: Meerminnen bestaan niet echt, hoor!). It is in this lesson because you will hear it every day.

Placement and stacking

Where. After the verb and the subject, before the thing you are talking about: Geef die boeken eens hier, Ik heb hem maar eens even geholpen. Never first in the sentence. Never stressed.

Stacking order. Strengtheners first (nou, toch), softeners after (maar, eens), and even always last. The ANS' full stack: Geef die boeken dan nou toch maar eens even hier. Nobody says all six. Two is natural: Kom maar even. Kijk eens even. Source: ANS 21.4.9.4.2.

Register. These belong in speech, texts and friendly emails. In a formal letter (lesson 0003) leave them out.
