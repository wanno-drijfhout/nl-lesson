Why this lesson

A phone call is the hardest talk in a new language. You cannot see a face. You cannot point. The other person speaks fast and reads numbers in a rush. And in the Netherlands, many things still start with a call: the doctor, the landlord, the gemeente.

This lesson gives you the fixed shape of a Dutch call. If you know the shape, you only have to listen for the new parts.

The idea: say your name first

In the Netherlands, the person who picks up says their name at once. No hallo. Just met + name:

— Met Jansen.
— Hallo Frank, met Jasmien.

Onze Taal describes these two habits (2007). The Dutch, like other northern countries, say their name. The French, Russians, Japanese and many southern countries say only hallo or ja, and wait.

Met here is short for u spreekt met, 'you are speaking with'. In a work call, the full form is common: Goedemorgen, u spreekt met J. de Vries. The Flemish language service gives this as the usual phrase (Team Taaladvies).

Ukrainian hook. In Ukrainian you pick up with Алло? or Так? and wait. That is the 'hallo' habit. In Dutch, a lone Hallo? sounds like you are not sure anyone is there. Say your name instead. It feels too open at first. It is normal here.

Answering with your name is the Dutch custom, not a rule. With a number you do not know, many people now just say Hallo? or their first name. The Onze Taal piece is a debate about exactly that.

Six moves

Almost every call to an office has these six steps. Learn one line for each.

MoveSayNote
1. NameGoedemorgen, u spreekt met J. de Vries.
Goedemiddag, met De Vries.		The short form is fine too. Say it slowly. Your name is new to them.
2. ReasonIk bel over mijn afspraak.
Ik bel omdat de verwarming niet werkt.		Ik bel over + thing, or ik bel omdat + sentence. Say it in the first breath.
3. AskIk wil graag een afspraak maken.
Kan ik iemand spreken over de huur?		Ik wil graag is polite and normal. Not rude, as 'I want' can be in English.
4. Not heardSorry, wilt u dat even herhalen?
Ik heb het laatste niet goed verstaan.
De verbinding is slecht.		All three are in the Team Taaladvies tips. They say: only say Hallo? when you think the line is dead.
5. Spell and numbersDe Vries. D van Dirk, E van Eduard, V van Victor …See the box below.
6. CloseDank u wel. Fijne dag nog!
Prima, dan zie ik u dinsdag.		Sum up the time or the next step, then thank them.

The six moves, the lines for moves 2, 3 and 6 and the notes on them are the teacher's own, from daily use.

Spelling your name. Dutch has its own phone alphabet. You say a letter, then van + a name: A van Anton, B van Bernard. Onze Taal prints this list (pdf) and notes that more than one version is in use (2014).

A Anton · B Bernard · C Cornelis · D Dirk · E Eduard · F Ferdinand · G Gerard · H Hendrik · I Izaak · J Jan · K Karel · L Lodewijk · M Marie · N Nico · O Otto · P Pieter · Q Quirinus · R Rudolf · S Simon · T Teunis · U Utrecht · V Victor · W Willem · X Xantippe · IJ IJmuiden · Y Ypsilon · Z Zaandam

Note IJ: it gets its own name, IJmuiden. For the Dutch, ij is close to one letter.

Numbers. Many Dutch people read a phone number in pairs: 06 · 12 · 34 · 56 · 78 becomes nul zes, twaalf, vierendertig, zesenvijftig, achtenzeventig. In 1978 a reader of Onze Taal wanted one digit at a time, as the old PTT rule said. He admits that in practice people almost always read pairs (Onze Taal 1978). Pairs are hard for you, because Dutch says the ones first: vierendertig is 'four-and-thirty'. So ask: Wilt u het cijfer voor cijfer zeggen?

Look at terecht kunnen: U kunt morgen terecht = 'you can come in tomorrow, there is a slot'. You hear it at every office. And Beterschap! = 'get well soon'. Say it to anyone who is ill.

Register. At an office, use u, even if the assistant uses a first name. When a friend calls, keep it short: — Met Daan. — Hé Daan, met Julia! Heb je even? Heb je even? = 'do you have a minute?'
