Glossary

Terms used across lessons. Every lesson uses these words in this sense.

Register
How formal a piece of language is. Informal (je, hoi, fixen) versus formal (u, geachte, repareren). Ukrainian ти/ви is the same idea.
V2, inversion
In a Dutch main clause the finite verb is always the second element. Put a time word first and the subject moves behind the verb: Morgen kook ik.
Present tense, tegenwoordige tijd (o.t.t.)
The plain verb form (ik kook). Dutch uses it for now and for later.
Auxiliary verb, hulpwerkwoord
A helper verb with an infinitive at the end: Ik ga koken. Ik zal koken.
Infinitive, infinitief
The dictionary form, ending in -en: koken, eten, gaan.
Calque
A phrase translated word for word from another language. Grammatical but not native: kom bij mijn huis for "come to my house".
Interference
A habit from another language leaking into Dutch. For you: English "will", German werden and Samstag.
Collocation
Words that go together by convention: een besluit nemen, never maken. Taaladvies. See collocations.
Salutation and closing, aanhef and slotgroet
The opening line (Geachte heer Jansen,) and sign-off (Met vriendelijke groet,) of a letter. They must match in register. See the formal email.
Particle, partikel, ANS: schakeringspartikel
Small flavour words that carry tone, not meaning: even, toch, hoor, wel, maar. The sound of native informal Dutch. See particles.
Tussen-s
The linking s inside a compound: verwarming + systeem = verwarmingssysteem. Taaladvies.
CEFR
Common European Framework of Reference. Levels A1, A2, B1, B2, C1, C2. Your placement: B2, partial C1.
